\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced scVICAR, which trains a masked autoencoder to recover cells from
locally mixed inputs. scVICAR-F uses a fixed mixing coefficient, and scVICAR-T
adapts edge weights and mixing strength to local graph structure. scVICAR-T
and scVICAR-F ranked first and second across four mean metrics in the
15-dataset development benchmark. scVICAR-T also achieved the highest mean ARI in the
frozen external comparison. Matched controls found smaller,
dataset-dependent effects from corruption design. Under controlled edge
replacement, the adaptive scores identified inaccurate neighbors, while the
resulting ARI changes varied by dataset. Low-label probes showed small positive
changes, and marker-based endpoints were heterogeneous. These results define
the empirical scope of neighbor-mixed anchor recovery for single-cell
representation learning.
