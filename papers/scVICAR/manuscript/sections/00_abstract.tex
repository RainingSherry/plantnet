Set-structured tabular data --- cells in a tissue, points in a cloud, image
patches, or token sets in long documents --- comes with an estimated local
neighborhood that conveys both signal and risk. We introduce
\emph{Anchor Recovery}, a self-supervised pre-training principle that
(i) constructs each training view as a bounded, cell-specific perturbation
toward selected neighbors, (ii) masks the perturbed view, and (iii) trains a
shared encoder to reconstruct the \emph{unchanged original} object. The
perturbation magnitude is data-dependent and the deployed encoder takes only
the raw object, discarding the graph after training.

We prove that anchor reconstruction bounds the input displacement and that
a Lipschitz encoder gap is controlled by the cross-class neighbor mass and
the mixing coefficient. Across $15$ development datasets, three seeds and
$16$ compared configurations, Anchor Recovery ranks first on every reported
clustering metric.
On five source-independent frozen datasets, it transfers as the top mean
ARI ($0.577$), NMI ($0.709$) and macro-F1 ($0.603$). Matched-backbone
ablations isolate the contributions of bounded perturbation, cell-specific
step size, and topology-informed affinity; their dataset-level permutation
statistics, including null effects and Holm-adjusted $p$-values, are reported
in full. Under controlled cross-class edge injection, the topology-informed
affinity detects contamination with mean AUROC $0.918$ and the cell-specific
step size tracks weighted neighborhood purity with mean Spearman $0.520$.
Code, frozen manifests, and complete run records are released.
