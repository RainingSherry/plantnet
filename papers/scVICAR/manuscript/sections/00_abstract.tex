\begin{abstract}
Masked autoencoding learns single-cell representations from gene-replacement
corruption but does not explicitly exploit local relationships among cells.  We
introduce scVICAR, a graph-vicinal anchor-recovery framework that reconstructs
an original cell from a bounded neighborhood-mixed view.  scVICAR-F uses a
fixed local perturbation, whereas scVICAR-T combines topology-informed affinity
with a cell-wise perturbation budget intended to limit exposure to unreliable
edges.  We show that the augmentation remains in a local convex hull, derive
input and representation perturbation bounds, express it as one step of
nonuniform graph diffusion, and bound its sensitivity to cross-class neighbor
mass.  A frozen matched-backbone study evaluates six confirmatory scRNA-seq
datasets with three model seeds, component ablations, and controlled bad-edge
injection.  Biological utility is assessed through held-out marker recovery,
marker-overlap annotation, and low-label probing of frozen embeddings.  The
study is designed to test fixed vicinal recovery against scMAE and the adaptive
variant's robustness under topology contamination, without assuming that
adaptation is universally superior on clean graphs.  Quantitative statements
are generated only after the complete preregistered matrix and downstream
artifacts pass checksum, leakage, and traceability checks.
\end{abstract}
