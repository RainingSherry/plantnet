\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) resolves transcriptomes at cellular
resolution, but its analytical value depends on representations that preserve
biological identity while suppressing technical variation. This balance is
difficult because expression matrices are sparse, high-dimensional, and
strongly affected by sequencing depth, transient state, and class imbalance.
Errors in the learned geometry can merge rare populations or fragment
continuous biological programs~\cite{kiselev2019challenges}. Reliable
representation learning is therefore central to both cell clustering and
subsequent annotation.

Deep generative and autoencoding models learn nonlinear structure directly
from expression profiles~\cite{lopez2018scvi,tian2019scdeepcluster,tian2021scdcc}.
Masked autoencoding provides an especially direct self-supervised objective:
the model corrupts part of an observation and reconstructs its original
content~\cite{he2022mae}. scMAE adapts this principle to scRNA-seq through
gene-value replacement, mask prediction, and expression reconstruction
~\cite{fang2024scmae}. Its corruption process exploits within-cell gene
context. Local relationships among cells remain unused.

Intercellular context could provide a biologically structured training signal,
yet using it safely is the central technical challenge. Expression-derived
nearest-neighbor graphs can cross cell types, technical batches, transitional
states, and rare-population boundaries. Uniform neighborhood propagation may
then move a cell outside its local biological support and erase informative
variation. A useful local corruption must therefore limit how far each cell
moves and expose the effects of unreliable neighbors.

We address this problem with \emph{single-cell Vicinal Corruption with Anchor
Recovery} (scVICAR). The key idea is to use local cell geometry as a
training-time corruption distribution, while retaining the original cell as
the reconstruction target. Given a row-stochastic sampled neighborhood
operator $\widehat P$ and diagonal matrix $G$ of mixing coefficients, scVICAR constructs
\begin{equation}
 X^v=(I-G)X+G\widehat PX=X-G(I-\widehat P)X.
 \label{eq:intro-view}
\end{equation}
The resulting view is subjected to the original gene-replacement corruption.
The clean scMAE branch remains active. Final embeddings are extracted directly
from clean profiles. Mixup, by comparison, interpolates inputs and prediction targets together
~\cite{zhang2018mixup}.

scVICAR separates local mixing from topology weighting. scVICAR-F uses
a fixed mixing coefficient and provides the simplest test of graph-vicinal
anchor recovery. scVICAR-T adds topology-informed affinity based on cosine
similarity, mutual-KNN relations, and shared-neighbor overlap. It also assigns
an analytic cell-specific mixing coefficient from local graph coherence. The
affinity is an uncalibrated edge weight. scVICAR-T reduces mixing when local
graph coherence is weak.

This formulation connects method design to testable properties. Each vicinal
view remains inside the convex hull of its anchor and sampled neighbors. Its
displacement is bounded by the local radius and cell-specific coefficient. The same
operator is one step of nonuniform graph diffusion, while a contamination bound
links perturbation size to cross-class neighbor mass. The stress experiments
measure these quantities under deliberate graph corruption.

We evaluate scVICAR in a broad development benchmark and a separately frozen
six-dataset study. The frozen study includes external baselines,
matched-backbone controls, bad-edge interventions, component ablations, marker
tasks, and low-label probes. Dataset is the independent statistical unit, and
every seed is retained. Labels are reserved for evaluation and the isolated
stress intervention.

The study makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
 \item We introduce graph-vicinal anchor recovery, which uses local cell
 geometry as bounded input corruption while preserving the original cell as
 the self-supervised target. The formulation covers fixed and topology-adaptive
 mixing without changing clean-profile inference.
 \item We derive convex-hull, perturbation, diffusion, and contamination
 properties. The adaptive design separates topology-informed affinity from a
 cell-specific mixing coefficient, enabling direct component and bad-edge tests.
 \item We evaluate 16 primary method configurations on 15 development datasets
 and test the frozen final variants on six additional datasets. Matched
 ablations, graph stress, marker tasks, and low-label probes distinguish
 competitiveness, attribution, graph-risk response, and downstream utility.
\end{itemize}
