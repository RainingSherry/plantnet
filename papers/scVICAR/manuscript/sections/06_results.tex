\section{Clustering and Robustness Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Large-Scale Benchmark across 15 Datasets}

The primary development comparison reports 16 method configurations on 15
datasets with three seeds per configuration and dataset. All ranked
configurations completed 45 runs. Table
~\ref{tab:development-benchmark} reports ACC, NMI, ARI, and macro-F1 over the
complete 720-run primary comparison. The exploratory contrastive extension is
retained in the supplement but omitted here because it did not improve the final model.

\input{../tables/development_valid15/development_benchmark_main}

The comparison spans classical community detection, PCA, ensemble clustering,
deep embedding, generative, graph-based, masked, and contrastive methods.
scVICAR-T ranked first on the mean of all four metrics, and scVICAR-F ranked
second on all four. The strongest non-scVICAR method, scNAME, reached 0.7326
mean ARI; scVICAR-T and scVICAR-F were 8.59\% and 7.40\% higher, respectively.
Against the direct scMAE parent, mean ARI increased from 0.7570 to 0.7955 for
scVICAR-T and 0.7868 for scVICAR-F, corresponding to relative improvements of
5.08\% and 3.93\%.

Dataset-level averages showed that scVICAR-T exceeded scMAE on 10 of 15
datasets, with a median ARI change of $+0.0152$. It ranked among the top three
methods on 11 datasets and ranked first on four. scVICAR-F exceeded scMAE on
9 of 15 datasets, with a median ARI change of $+0.0019$. Its largest ARI gains
occurred on Shekhar and Macosko, and several neuronal datasets favored
scMAE. The benchmark shows broad performance together with the datasets where
local mixing was less effective. The configurations in this
table were finalized during benchmark development.

\input{../tables/development_valid15/development_principal_dataset_ari}

Table~\ref{tab:development-dataset-ari} exposes the dataset-level variation
behind the mean ranking. The largest gains occurred on Shekhar, Macosko, Baron,
and Quake Smart-seq2 Lung. Bach, Young, and worm neuron cell illustrate that
topology adaptation was not uniformly beneficial.

\subsection{Frozen External Competitiveness}

We next evaluated the frozen variants on six additional datasets. The
comparison retained every dataset and all three seeds. All values came from the
frozen reruns.

\input{generated/baseline_results}

scVICAR-T achieved the highest mean ARI in this external comparison. Its mean
ARI of 0.617 exceeded Original scMAE by 2.13\%, PCA+KMeans by 5.11\%, scDCC by
11.17\%, scDeepCluster by 21.46\%, scRCL by 17.75\%, and scVI by 44.16\%.
scVICAR-F achieved the highest mean NMI and macro-F1. These rankings show
competitive absolute performance under the frozen benchmark. This comparison
ranks complete methods with different backbones, objectives, and
implementations. The matched analysis below isolates corruption design.

\subsection{Matched-Backbone Attribution}

We then isolated corruption design by holding preprocessing, architecture,
optimizer, training budget, and seed schedule fixed. All \FormalRunCount{}

dataset-level seed averages from the prespecified aggregation.

The matched effects were substantially smaller than the cross-method ranking.
scVICAR-F changed mean ARI by $-0.002$ relative to NoMix and improved on one of
six datasets. scVICAR-T changed mean ARI by $+0.005$ and improved on three of
six datasets. Both dataset-level intervals included zero. Human Pancreas 3
showed the largest adaptive gain, whereas several datasets favored NoMix.
The external ranking reflects each complete training pipeline. The matched
analysis assigns only a small, dataset-dependent incremental effect to local
corruption within the common backbone.

\subsection{Component Evidence and Negative Controls}

The component variants clarified which parts of topology adaptation carried
the signal. Random mixing had the lowest descriptive mean ARI (0.594), below
NoMix (0.611), showing that arbitrary convex smoothing was insufficient.
Topology edge weighting alone also remained below NoMix (0.606). In contrast,
the gate-only variant attained the highest descriptive mean ARI (0.621),
followed by full scVICAR-T (0.617). The gate-only result points to the
cell-specific mixing coefficient as the more promising adaptive component under
clean graphs. Gate-only was outside the prespecified inferential contrasts, so its
ordering is reported as a descriptive component result.

Vicinal variants required about twice the runtime of NoMix in the current
implementation. Figure~\ref{fig:components} reports performance, runtime, and
representation geometry using the prespecified descriptive summaries.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Label Inclusion and Clustering Operator}

The primary cohort excluded unknown, unassigned, and very small classes. We
therefore repeated the principal NoMix/F/T comparison while retaining every
source label and cell. This 54-run sensitivity analysis was frozen separately
and was never pooled with the primary cohort.

\input{generated/full_label_sensitivity_results}

The complete-label result was directionally stronger for scVICAR-T. Its mean
ARI increased by 0.030 over NoMix and favored scVICAR-T on five of six
datasets, although the dataset-level interval still included zero. This pattern
suggests that topology adaptation may matter more when rare or unresolved
populations remain in the evaluation. A dedicated rare-cell study would be
needed to test that interpretation directly.

We also replaced known-$K$ KMeans with fixed-resolution Leiden to assess
dependence on the clustering operator.

\input{generated/leiden_results}

Absolute scores and method ordering changed under Leiden. The fixed variant
had a positive mean difference of 0.012 relative to NoMix, while the adaptive
difference was 0.002. Both intervals included zero. Because resolution and
neighborhood size were fixed before evaluation, this analysis contains no
label-guided resolution sweep.

\subsection{Topology Scores Detect Erroneous-Edge Risk}

The contamination analysis directly tested the quantities identified by the
theoretical bound. We replaced 0--100\% of each graph row with deliberately
cross-class neighbors on three datasets and retained the full degradation
curve.

\input{generated/stress_results}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../figures/final/fig4_graph_stress.pdf}
  \caption{Controlled graph contamination on three datasets with three model
  seeds per condition. (a) ARI as cross-class edge replacement increases.
  Faint lines denote datasets and bold lines denote means. (b) Difference
  between clean-referenced ARI responses of scVICAR-T and scVICAR-F. Positive
  values favor scVICAR-T. (c) Affinity AUROC for distinguishing retained
  same-class from injected cross-class edges. (d) Association between the
  cell-specific coefficient and weighted neighborhood purity. The diagnostic statistics
  are undefined at 100\% replacement because no same-class edges remain.}
  \label{fig:graph_stress}
\end{figure*}

Topology-informed affinity separated retained same-class edges from injected
cross-class edges with mean AUROC 0.918. The cell-specific coefficient also tracked
weighted neighborhood purity (mean Spearman 0.520). Both adaptive components
responded to their intended graph property. Clustering protection was less
consistent. At complete replacement,
scVICAR-T degraded more slowly on two datasets and scVICAR-F on one. The
topology scores therefore detected contamination. Their effect on clustering
depended on the dataset and corrupted geometry.
