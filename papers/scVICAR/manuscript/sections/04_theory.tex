\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

We characterize the augmentation operator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:unified-view}.
Throughout, $\widehat P$ is nonnegative and row-stochastic and
$0\leq g_i\leq1$. The analysis bounds the location and propagation of each
vicinal perturbation. Clustering effects are evaluated empirically. NoMix is
the degenerate case $G=0$, where the perturbation equals zero.

\begin{proposition}[Convex-hull preservation and displacement bound]
For each cell $i$,
\begin{equation}
 x_i^v\in\operatorname{conv}
 \bigl(\{x_i\}\cup\{x_j:\widehat p_{ij}>0\}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and, with $R_i=\max_{j:\widehat p_{ij}>0}\|x_j-x_i\|_2$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \|x_i^v-x_i\|_2
 &\leq g_i\sum_j\widehat p_{ij}\|x_j-x_i\|_2\\
 &\leq g_{\max}R_i .
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:radius-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The coefficients of $x_i$ and its sampled neighbors in
$x_i^v=(1-g_i)x_i+g_i\sum_j\widehat p_{ij}x_j$ are nonnegative and sum to one.
Moreover,
$x_i^v-x_i=g_i\sum_j\widehat p_{ij}(x_j-x_i)$; the triangle inequality,
row-stochasticity, and $g_i\leq g_{\max}$ yield
Eq.~\eqref{eq:radius-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Lipschitz propagation]
If encoder $h$ is $L_h$-Lipschitz on the local convex hull, then
\begin{equation}
 \bigl\|h(x_i^v)-h(x_i)\bigr\|_2
 \leq L_h\,g_iR_i.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
This corollary bounds how input displacement propagates through the encoder.

\begin{proposition}[One-step nonuniform graph diffusion]
Let $L_{\mathrm{rw}}=I-\widehat P$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 X^v=X-GL_{\mathrm{rw}}X.
 \label{eq:diffusion}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expanding the right-hand side gives
$X-GX+G\widehat PX=(I-G)X+G\widehat PX$.
\end{proof}
Thus the view is one explicit Euler step with a node-dependent step size.  In
the additional special case $G=gI$ and a symmetric (or suitably reversible)
kernel, an eigencomponent of $L_{\mathrm{rw}}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_r$ is
multiplied by $1-g\lambda_r$. This supplies a conditional low-pass
interpretation and the familiar oversmoothing caution~\cite{li2018laplacian}.
scVICAR applies one such step to each training view.

\begin{proposition}[Log-linear topology kernel]
Under $d_{ij}=1-s_{ij}$, positive factors
$1+\gamma_m m_{ij}>0$ and $1+\gamma_q q_{ij}^{\mathrm{SNN}}>0$, and on edges
where the implementation's multiplicative-factor clipping is inactive,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:topology-affinity} is equivalent to
\begin{align}
 r_{ij}&=(\tau^{-1}+\gamma_s+\gamma_d)s_{ij}\nonumber\\
 &\quad+\log(1+\gamma_m m_{ij})
 +\log(1+\gamma_q q_{ij}^{\mathrm{SNN}}),\nonumber\\
 a_{ij}&=\frac{\exp(r_{ij})}
 {\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal N_i}\exp(r_{i\ell})}.
 \label{eq:loglinear}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Taking logarithms of the unnormalized affinity, substituting
$p_{ij}^{(0)}\propto\exp(s_{ij}/\tau)$ and $d_{ij}=1-s_{ij}$, and deleting the
row-constant term $-\gamma_d$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:loglinear}.
\end{proof}
The affinity is therefore a product-of-experts, or log-linear, graph score.
Cosine similarity and cosine distance are algebraically redundant here. The
resulting edge weights are uncalibrated.

\begin{proposition}[Cross-neighborhood contamination]
Partition sampled neighbors into a same-class set $S_i$ and a cross-class set
$C_i$, only for post-hoc analysis.  Define
$\eta_i=\sum_{j\in C_i}\widehat p_{ij}$ and assume
$\|x_j-x_i\|_2\leq R_i^{\mathrm{in}}$ on $S_i$ and
$\|x_j-x_i\|_2\leq R_i^{\mathrm{out}}$ on $C_i$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \|x_i^v-x_i\|_2\leq
 g_i\left[(1-\eta_i)R_i^{\mathrm{in}}+
 \eta_iR_i^{\mathrm{out}}\right].
 \label{eq:contamination}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Split $g_i\sum_j\widehat p_{ij}(x_j-x_i)$ over $S_i$ and $C_i$, apply the
triangle inequality and the two radius assumptions, and use the respective
weight masses $1-\eta_i$ and $\eta_i$.
\end{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:contamination} connects the adaptive terms to measurable
graph properties. Holding the radii and coefficient fixed, reducing $\eta_i$
monotonically tightens the bound when
$R_i^{\mathrm{out}}\geq R_i^{\mathrm{in}}$. Reducing $g_i$ contracts the
entire perturbation. An adaptive advantage requires the affinity or mixing
coefficient to respond favorably to inaccurate neighbors. Controlled bad-edge
injection and edge-purity diagnostics measure this response.

\subsection{Local Expansion of the Auxiliary Loss}

For fixed model parameters, a fixed minibatch $B$ containing cell $i$, and
fixed context $X_{B\setminus i}$, let $X_B^{(i\leftarrow v)}$ denote the batch
obtained by replacing row $i$ with $v$.  Because the implementation draws one
shared permutation for the entire batch, the relevant corruption-averaged
anchor risk is the batch-conditional function
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{i,B}(v;X_{B\setminus i})=\mathbb E_{M,\Pi}
 \!\left[\ell_\theta\!\left(
 [C_{M,\Pi}(X_B^{(i\leftarrow v)})]_i,x_i\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Let $\delta_i(\widehat P)=\sum_j\widehat p_{ij}x_j-x_i$, where $\widehat P$
also varies with the implemented neighbor sampler.  If the batch-conditional
$\Phi_{i,B}$ is three times continuously differentiable in the local hull and
its third derivative is bounded, suppress the fixed context by writing
$\phi_i(v)=\Phi_{i,B}(v;X_{B\setminus i})$.  Multivariate Taylor expansion gives
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E_{\widehat P}\phi_i(x_i+g_i\delta_i)
 &=\phi_i(x_i)
 +g_i\nabla_v\phi_i(x_i)^\top
   \mathbb E_{\widehat P}\delta_i \nonumber\\
 &\quad+\frac{g_i^2}{2}\operatorname{tr}
 \left(H_i\mathbb E_{\widehat P}[\delta_i\delta_i^\top]\right)
 +O\!\left(g_i^3\mathbb E_{\widehat P}\|\delta_i\|_2^3\right).
 \label{eq:taylor}
\end{align}
The first-order term is small under an additional condition such as an
approximately centered sampled perturbation or a small input-direction
gradient $\nabla_v\phi_i(x_i)$. Stationarity with respect to model parameters
concerns a different derivative. Under the stated condition, the
second-order term admits a graph-supported curvature-regularization
interpretation.  Only under the further approximation of a small residual for a squared
reconstruction loss does this resemble a directional Jacobian penalty.
Equation~\eqref{eq:taylor} provides a local interpretation of the auxiliary
loss; empirical comparisons determine its clustering effect.

The propositions motivate three measurements. RandomMix tests the contribution
of local geometry. Cross-class edge replacement tests sensitivity to erroneous
weight mass. Gate--purity association tests whether cell-specific mixing tracks
local graph quality. The analysis assumes a given KNN graph and an unspecified
local encoder Lipschitz constant. The confirmatory and downstream experiments
measure the learned representation directly.
