\section{Downstream Predictive Utility and Marker Coherence}
\label{sec:downstream}

Clustering agreement measures partition quality. We additionally evaluate
low-label linear separability with frozen probes and cluster-level gene programs
with marker tasks. Evaluation labels are reserved for final scoring.

\subsection{Marker Recovery}

For each dataset and split seed $s\in\{11,23,37,53,71\}$, we stratify cells into
50\% reference and 50\% evaluation subsets.  Reference markers are the top 100
Wilcoxon-ranked genes for each true type using reference cells only.  Evaluation
markers are independently ranked for each predicted cluster. True evaluation
labels are opened after differential expression. Type--cluster overlap matrices
yield Recovery@20, @50, and @100, Jaccard similarity, overlap coefficient, and
the fraction of evaluation clusters with fewer than three valid markers.
Unmatched reference types receive zero recovery and remain in the mean.

\subsection{Marker-Overlap Annotation}

Each predicted cluster is assigned the reference type with maximum marker
overlap; multiple clusters may map to the same type.  Ties are resolved by mean
shared-marker log fold change and then lexicographically.  A cluster with fewer
than three markers or zero maximum overlap is \texttt{unassigned}.  Evaluation
cells are scored by accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro-F1, per-class recall, and
coverage.  Hungarian mapping is reported only as an oracle upper bound and is
never described as a practical annotation method.

\subsection{Low-Label Frozen-Embedding Probe}

At 10\% and 30\% label fractions, a stratified subset trains
\texttt{StandardScaler} followed by L2 multinomial logistic regression
($C=1$, balanced class weights, 2,000 maximum iterations).  The scaler and
classifier are fit only on the training subset; the encoder remains frozen.
We report accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro-F1, and per-class recall over the
five fixed split seeds. The task measures within-dataset transductive
annotation.

\subsection{Low-Label Signal Is Consistent but Small}

The most consistent downstream signal occurred in frozen low-label probes. At
10\% labels, scVICAR-F improved on NoMix in all six datasets and scVICAR-T in
five. Their relative macro-F1 gains were 0.29\% and 0.34\%, respectively. At
30\% labels, the corresponding gains were 0.13\% and 0.22\%. These effects are
directionally consistent but small, and none remained significant after Holm
correction. Marker-based endpoints provided a different result. scVICAR-T had
negative mean changes in Recovery@100 and marker-annotation macro-F1, with both
intervals spanning zero. Modest changes in linear separability therefore
coexisted with heterogeneous cluster-level marker coherence.

\subsection{Human Pancreas 3 Biological Case Study}

The case study fixes scMAE/NoMix, scVICAR-F, and scVICAR-T before inspecting
markers.  It combines a marker dot or track plot, type--cluster overlap heatmap,
and a reference-type $\rightarrow$ predicted-cluster $\rightarrow$ marker
annotation $\rightarrow$ gold-type Sankey diagram.  A literature-verified
pancreatic marker panel is used only for interpretation. The panel is frozen from the
primary human-pancreas atlas literature, principally Baron et
al.~\cite{baron2016pancreas,segerstolpe2016pancreas}.  We distinguish the 8,629
human cells reported after quality control in the primary Baron article, the
8,569-cell benchmark input, and the 8,562 cells retained by our prespecified
filter. These values correspond to three processing stages.
Figure~\ref{fig:pancreas-case} reports the prespecified seed and split.
All downstream computations reuse the frozen embeddings. Split-level outputs
remain linked to their originating run identifiers, which prevents favorable
seed or split selection.

Across all model and split seeds for Human Pancreas 3, scVICAR-T increased
Recovery@100 from 0.616 for NoMix to 0.623. Marker-overlap annotation macro-F1
remained essentially unchanged (0.719 for NoMix and 0.718 for scVICAR-T).
The 10\% and 30\% probes increased from 0.945 to 0.949 and from 0.954 to
0.956, respectively. The case therefore illustrates the broader result:
neighbor mixing produced a small improvement in label-efficient linear
separation without a corresponding uniform gain in marker-based annotation.
