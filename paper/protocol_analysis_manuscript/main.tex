\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Motivation.}
Masked autoencoding has recently been used for single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) clustering by corrupting gene expression, predicting the corrupted entries, and reconstructing the original expression profile. The representative scMAE model uses gene-wise shuffled corruption and a mask-prediction decoder to learn embeddings that are then clustered downstream. This design addresses an important limitation of earlier autoencoder, graph, and contrastive methods: gene-gene dependency is made part of the representation-learning task. It leaves a second question unresolved. When a clustering objective is placed on top of a masked autoencoder, the learned latent space can change in ways that reconstruction loss and assignment loss do not expose.
\textbf{Results.}
We study this question through a zero-mask variant of scMAE coupled with Deep Embedded Clustering (DEC). In same-harness diagnostics, DEC sharpening on Macosko retinal data reduces the usable per-coordinate variance of the latent code and yields poor known-$K$ clustering. VarFloor-scMAE keeps the scMAE mask-prediction and reconstruction structure, replaces global shuffled corruption with zero corruption, adds DEC after a warmup period, and imposes a per-dimension standard-deviation floor on the DEC latent. In a completed 23-dataset, three-seed known-$K$ benchmark, VarFloor-scMAE improves over PCA+$k$-means on 18 datasets, with mean ARI $0.5938$ compared with $0.5225$ for PCA+$k$-means. In the 18 datasets where published scMAE-family baselines are also available, VarFloor-scMAE is not uniformly superior to scMAE, but it improves strongly over standalone DEC and remains competitive with stronger deep baselines. The contribution is therefore bounded: masked reconstruction can provide a useful encoder, DEC can collapse or reshape its coordinate geometry, and a per-dimension floor is a reproducible correction that improves the formal known-$K$ baseline without establishing a universally dominant clustering method.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Single-cell RNA sequencing converts tissue into a sparse, high-dimensional expression matrix whose rows are cells and whose columns are genes. Clustering this matrix supports cell-type discovery, atlas construction, rare-population analysis, and downstream marker interpretation \citep{kiselev2019challenges}. Although clustering is unsupervised during training, methods are usually judged after the fact against curated labels. This evaluation setting places the main burden on representation learning: a method must produce a geometry in which biological groups are separable, while the protocol must prevent label information from entering through cluster-count selection, resolution tuning, early stopping, or hyperparameter search.

Classical workflows remain strong under this burden. Log-normalization, highly variable gene selection, principal-component analysis (PCA), and $k$-means or graph clustering are still central in Scanpy- and Seurat-style pipelines \citep{wolf2018scanpy,stuart2019seurat}. PCA is not only a historical baseline. It preserves dominant expression variance before any nonlinear objective can distort the geometry, and it often reveals whether deep feature learning has improved or damaged a separable structure. A fair deep clustering study must therefore treat PCA+$k$-means as a required comparator rather than as an intentionally weak baseline.

Deep single-cell clustering methods attempt to replace or refine this explicit pipeline. DEC introduced a widely used template: learn an embedding, compute soft assignments to centroids, sharpen the assignments, and optimize a KL divergence toward the sharpened target \citep{xie2016dec}. Single-cell variants combine this idea with count models, autoencoders, graph propagation, biological constraints, or contrastive objectives \citep{tian2019scdeepcluster,li2020desc,tian2021scdcc,wang2021scgnn,gan2022scdsc,wan2022scname,xu2024sccdcg}. These methods make clustering part of representation learning, but the same coupling creates a risk. A clustering loss can improve assignment confidence while compressing the latent space needed to separate fine cell states.

scMAE approaches the problem from a different direction. It corrupts gene expression by gene-wise shuffling, predicts which entries were corrupted, and reconstructs the original expression matrix from the learned embedding and predicted mask \citep{fang2024scmae}. Its narrative is precise: existing methods do not sufficiently exploit gene correlations; corruption plus mask prediction forces the encoder to capture these correlations; the resulting embeddings improve clustering and rare-cell identification across many datasets. The core formulas reflect that narrative: a Bernoulli mask selects entries, shuffled donor values replace those entries, the encoder produces a low-dimensional embedding, a mask predictor estimates the corruption pattern, and a weighted reconstruction loss trains the decoder.

VarFloor-scMAE starts from this structure but asks a narrower question. The question is not whether masked autoencoding can learn gene-gene dependency. It is whether the embedding remains geometrically usable after a clustering objective is added. To isolate this question, we use zero corruption rather than global shuffled corruption, retain the mask-prediction and reconstruction pathway, add DEC after warmup, and monitor the standard deviation of each latent coordinate. This route differs from scMAE in three ways: the corruption is made donor-free, the clustering objective is optimized jointly rather than applied only after embedding extraction, and the latent geometry is explicitly constrained.

The design was shaped by a sequence of controlled failures. Adding expression-derived pseudo-structure through neighbor mixing, gene programs, marker masks, module attention, or reliability heads did not produce a stable main method. A gene-program bottleneck could even perform worse than a row-permuted program target, indicating that the semantic auxiliary target pulled the embedding toward non-cell-type axes. Decoder-bypass ablations showed that sending mask logits to the decoder is not merely a shortcut; it stabilizes training on fine-grained data, although a high-ARI Macosko basin is reachable in rare seeds without the full bypass. Corruption ablations showed that global shuffled corruption, the defining perturbation in scMAE, can be harmful on fine-grained Macosko, while nuisance-matched shuffling can reach a higher Macosko basin but does not transfer to Melanoma, Quake, or the tested plant datasets. These findings narrow the stable claim. The robust cross-dataset spine is not another source of expression-derived structure. It is zero-mask scMAE, DEC refinement, and a direct constraint on latent coordinate activity.

This paper therefore treats VarFloor-scMAE as a benchmarked diagnostic method rather than as a broad winner claim. On Macosko retinal data \citep{macosko2015dropseq}, zero-mask scMAE plus DEC without the floor uses far fewer active latent dimensions and obtains about $0.33$ ARI under a clean three-seed mechanism diagnostic. Adding a per-coordinate standard-deviation floor raises the minimum coordinate standard deviation above the chosen threshold, increases participation-ratio effective dimension, and raises ARI to about $0.70$ on seeds 42--44. The completed formal benchmark then changes the claim boundary: across 23 datasets and seeds 42, 2024, and 3407, VarFloor-scMAE beats PCA+$k$-means on most datasets, but it does not dominate published scMAE-family methods in their 18-dataset overlap. The supported contribution is specific: DEC-style sharpening can induce per-dimension variance collapse in a masked scRNA autoencoder, and a standard-deviation floor is an auditable correction that is useful under a reproducible known-$K$ protocol.

The paper makes four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It separates the contribution of scMAE from the contribution of VarFloor-scMAE: scMAE uses masked reconstruction to learn gene-dependent embeddings, whereas VarFloor-scMAE studies how DEC changes the geometry of such embeddings.
  \item It defines a zero-mask scMAE + DEC objective with a per-dimension standard-deviation floor, including the formulas needed to identify which part of the loss addresses which failure.
  \item It reports same-harness evidence that the floor restores latent coordinate usage in a Macosko mechanism diagnostic and improves over PCA+$k$-means on 18 of 23 formal benchmark datasets.
  \item It records design constraints from full-benchmark and ablation evidence: PCA is a strong baseline, scMAE-family baselines remain competitive, and global shuffling, nuisance-matched shuffling, decoder bypass, neighbor mixing, and gene-program targets are not the promoted mechanism.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}

\subsection{scRNA-seq clustering as a representation protocol}

Given an expression matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times G}$, the formal output of clustering is a partition of $N$ cells. The practical object optimized by most methods is not the partition itself but a transformed representation of $X$. Sparsity, count-depth variation, batch effects, rare populations, and continuous differentiation trajectories make distances in the raw matrix unreliable \citep{kiselev2019challenges}. Standard pipelines therefore normalize counts, select highly variable genes, reduce dimension, construct a graph or run $k$-means, and consult labels only for final reporting.

This protocol view matters because it changes how improvements should be interpreted. SC3, Louvain, Leiden, Scanpy, and Seurat-style workflows expose their assumptions through feature selection, dimensionality reduction, graph construction, and resolution choices \citep{kiselev2017sc3,blondel2008louvain,traag2019leiden,wolf2018scanpy,stuart2019seurat}. Deep methods should meet the same standard. A higher ARI is not informative unless the preprocessing, seed set, cluster-count policy, and model-selection rule are fixed before labels are consulted. Benchmarks such as scCluBench reinforce this point by comparing traditional, graph-based, deep, and foundation-model methods under unified protocols \citep{xu2026scclubench}.

\subsection{Protocol leakage and cluster-count discipline}

The present manuscript separates known-$K$ diagnostics from unknown-$K$ model selection. Supplying the true number of clusters to $k$-means can be useful when the question is whether an embedding contains separable cell-type information, but it is not a fully unsupervised discovery protocol. Conversely, selecting a Leiden resolution by silhouette can be misleading on imbalanced scRNA-seq data: a coarse partition that captures the dominant population may score well while missing fine-grained subtypes. The disciplined comparison is therefore to fix the protocol before labels are inspected, report known-$K$ and inferred-$K$ settings separately, and treat labels only as final evaluation targets.

\subsection{Original scMAE contract}

scMAE is built around a reconstruction task designed for gene dependency. For each gene $j$, the original method forms a shuffled donor column $X'_{\cdot j}$ by permuting the values of $X_{\cdot j}$ across cells. It then samples a Bernoulli mask
\begin{equation}
M_{ij} \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_j)
\end{equation}
and constructs the corrupted matrix
\begin{equation}
X^{\mathrm{swap}}_{ij}
= (1-M_{ij})X_{ij} + M_{ij}X'_{ij}.
\end{equation}
An encoder maps the corrupted cell vector to an embedding,
\begin{equation}
z_i = f_{\theta}(X^{\mathrm{swap}}_{i\cdot}),
\end{equation}
a mask predictor estimates logits $a_i=g_{\theta}(z_i)$, and a decoder reconstructs the target expression from the concatenated vector $[z_i,a_i]$. With a reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{r}$ and mask-prediction loss $\mathcal{L}_{m}$, scMAE optimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scMAE}}
= (1-\gamma)\mathcal{L}_{r} + \gamma\mathcal{L}_{m}.
\end{equation}
The learned encoder embedding is then clustered by $k$-means or Leiden.

The important point is structural. In scMAE, the mask predictor and decoder make reconstruction aware of the corrupted entries, and the clustering phase is downstream of the learned embedding. The published evidence then follows a performance-and-biology form: compare against deep single-cell clustering methods across many datasets, analyze component ablations, examine rare-cell recovery, and validate biological interpretation through markers and enrichment. VarFloor-scMAE does not reproduce that claim pattern. It tests the additional condition required when clustering is optimized during representation learning: the latent space must keep enough active coordinates for DEC to separate fine-grained cell states.

\subsection{DEC sharpening and its geometric risk}

DEC optimizes assignments in a learned latent space \citep{xie2016dec}. Given embeddings $z_i$ and centroids $\mu_k$, it computes Student-$t$ soft assignments
\begin{equation}
q_{ik} =
\frac{(1 + \|z_i-\mu_k\|^2)^{-1}}
{\sum_{k'}(1 + \|z_i-\mu_{k'}\|^2)^{-1}}.
\end{equation}
The assignments are sharpened into a target distribution
\begin{equation}
p_{ik} =
\frac{q_{ik}^2 / \sum_i q_{ik}}
{\sum_{k'} q_{ik'}^2 / \sum_i q_{ik'}}
\end{equation}
and the model minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DEC}}
= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
p_{ik}\log\frac{p_{ik}}{q_{ik}}.
\end{equation}
This objective can make a reconstruction embedding more cluster-aligned. It can also make the embedding less usable if sharpening pulls cells toward a small set of dominant directions. On imbalanced or fine-grained data, the loss may decrease while marginal coordinate variance disappears. The resulting problem is not only a poor clustering head. It is a latent geometry failure.

\subsection{Variance floors}

Representation collapse is usually discussed in self-supervised learning when an encoder maps inputs to identical or near-identical embeddings. VICReg prevents collapse by adding a variance term that keeps each coordinate above a standard-deviation threshold, alongside invariance and covariance terms for paired-view learning \citep{bardes2022vicreg}. VarFloor-scMAE imports only the marginal variance principle. It does not require negative samples, paired image views, or covariance whitening.

For a mini-batch $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d}$, define
\begin{equation}
\sigma_j(Z) = \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{i=1}^{B}(Z_{ij})+\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
The variance-floor loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vf}}(Z)
= \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\max(0,\tau-\sigma_j(Z)).
\end{equation}
The hinge form is important. Coordinates above the floor receive no additional expansion pressure, so the term does not try to maximize variance indefinitely. It targets the measured failure: inactive dimensions with low standard deviation. The present implementation uses $\tau=1$ and weight $0.02$.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Overview and relation to scMAE}

VarFloor-scMAE keeps the mask-prediction autoencoder form of scMAE but changes the role of clustering and the treatment of latent geometry, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:scmae-comparison}. The method has three inherited components: an encoder, a mask predictor, and a decoder trained against the original expression profile. It has three departures: zero-mask corruption, DEC refinement during training, and a per-coordinate standard-deviation floor on the DEC latent.

\subsection{Zero-mask masked autoencoding}

Let $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^{G}$ be the preprocessed expression vector for cell $i$. A Bernoulli mask $m_i \in \{0,1\}^{G}$ is sampled with probability $\rho$. Instead of drawing donor values from other cells, VarFloor-scMAE sets selected entries to zero:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{x}_i = (1-m_i)\odot x_i.
\end{equation}
This choice has two purposes. First, it makes the corruption operation independent of donor-cell selection, library-size matching, and nuisance-bin design. Second, it isolates the effect of the clustering objective. If DEC damages the latent space under this simple corruption, the failure cannot be attributed to global swap donors.

The encoder, mask predictor, and decoder are
\begin{align}
z_i &= f_{\theta}(\tilde{x}_i), \\
a_i &= g_{\theta}(z_i), \\
\hat{x}_i &= h_{\theta}([z_i,a_i]).
\end{align}
The decoder receives mask logits, following the scMAE design. This path is retained because diagnostic ablations do not support treating it as a removable shortcut. Removing or compressing the mask path can reach a high Macosko basin in isolated seeds, but it is unstable; with the full mask path, the zero-mask DEC+floor configuration is reproducible across the clean three-seed diagnostic.

The reconstruction term uses entry weights derived from the mask,
\begin{equation}
\omega_{ij} = \eta m_{ij} + (1-\eta)(1-m_{ij}),
\end{equation}
and an elementwise reconstruction loss $\ell$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{r}
= \frac{1}{NG}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{G}
\omega_{ij}\ell(\hat{x}_{ij},x_{ij}).
\end{equation}
The mask-prediction term is binary cross-entropy,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{m}
= \frac{1}{NG}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{G}
\operatorname{BCE}(a_{ij},m_{ij}).
\end{equation}
The masked-autoencoding loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mae}}
= (1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{r}+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{m}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{DEC refinement after reconstruction warmup}

DEC is introduced only after a warmup period. This ordering preserves the role of masked reconstruction as the initial representation task and avoids assigning cluster targets before the encoder has learned a stable expression geometry. Cluster centers are initialized in the latent space, assignments $q_{ik}$ and targets $p_{ik}$ are updated periodically, and the KL loss is applied with an optional confidence mask:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dec}}
= \frac{1}{\sum_i c_i}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_i\sum_{k=1}^{K}
p_{ik}\log\frac{p_{ik}}{q_{ik}},
\qquad
c_i=\mathbb{I}\{\max_k p_{ik}\geq \kappa\}.
\end{equation}
The cluster count $K$ is supplied by the known-$K$ development protocol. Labels are not used for training or target construction.

\subsection{Per-dimension standard-deviation floor}

The variance-floor term is applied to the same latent code used by DEC. This is deliberate. Earlier dual-subspace and gene-program-bottleneck variants moved active variation away from the clustering subspace and reduced known-$K$ performance. The floor therefore acts on the clustering latent itself:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vf}}
= \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\max(0,\tau-\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{i}(z_{ij})+\epsilon}).
\end{equation}
The term is not a biological prior. It does not claim that each coordinate corresponds to a cell type, marker program, or interpretable factor. It only enforces the minimum condition that each coordinate can vary across cells.

The final objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mae}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{dec}}s(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dec}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{vf}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vf}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{cons}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cons}},
\end{equation}
where $s(t)$ ramps DEC after warmup and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cons}}$ is a weak-view latent consistency term used in the runner. The promoted configuration fixes the sharp DEC path and omits the earlier adaptive sharp/soft switch.

\subsection{Latent-geometry diagnostics}

Clustering metrics alone do not identify the failure targeted by VarFloor-scMAE. We therefore report marginal standard deviations and participation-ratio effective dimension. If $s_j$ is the standard deviation of latent coordinate $j$ over the dataset, the effective dimension is
\begin{equation}
d_{\mathrm{eff}}
= \frac{(\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_j^2)^2}
{\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_j^4}.
\end{equation}
This statistic distinguishes a nominally high-dimensional latent with many inactive coordinates from a latent that uses its coordinate capacity more evenly.

\section{Experimental Protocol}

\subsection{Evaluation boundary}

The evidence reported here has two layers. The first is a completed same-harness known-$K$ benchmark for VarFloor-scMAE and PCA+$k$-means across 23 datasets and three seeds. The second is a set of mechanism diagnostics and ablations that explain why the promoted objective was chosen. Labels are used only after training for ARI, NMI, and related reporting metrics \citep{hubert1985comparing,vinh2010information}. Known-$K$ $k$-means is a controlled diagnostic because it separates representation quality from unknown-$K$ selection. It should not be read as a fully unsupervised deployment workflow or as evidence that the method solves biological annotation.

\subsection{Datasets, seeds, and comparators}

The formal benchmark uses 23 datasets and seeds 42, 2024, and 3407 for both newly registered full-coverage methods. This seed set is the basis for the main VarFloor-scMAE versus PCA+$k$-means comparison. The main mechanism diagnostic uses Macosko mouse retina because it is fine-grained and imbalanced \citep{macosko2015dropseq}. The clean variance-floor comparison uses seeds 42, 43, and 44 to isolate the unfloored DEC failure. The difference between this diagnostic seed set and the formal seed set is reported explicitly because Macosko is seed-sensitive. Melanoma\_5K, Quake\_10x\_Spleen, and plant datasets are used in the ablation narrative to test whether corruption and auxiliary-structure variants transfer beyond the dataset on which they first appeared promising.

The required comparators are PCA+$k$-means under identical preprocessing \citep{jolliffe2002pca,lloyd1982least,arthur2007kmeanspp}, zero-mask scMAE without DEC, zero-mask scMAE + DEC without the variance floor, VarFloor-scMAE, and selected ablations that test whether a proposed mechanism changes the same latent geometry. Historical benchmark rows with partial coverage are used only through overlap comparisons. PCA is included even when a deep method is the focus because a nonlinear objective should not destroy separability already available in a linear subspace.

\subsection{The effect is not a readout artifact}

Head re-evaluation on frozen embeddings rules out a simple explanation in which the observed gain comes only from $k$-means. The unfloored DEC embedding is weak across evaluated heads and resolutions, whereas the DEC+floor embedding remains stronger under the same readout family. Leiden resolution sweeps also expose a protocol trap: silhouette-based resolution selection on imbalanced scRNA-seq can prefer coarse partitions that inflate ARI by capturing dominant cell classes rather than matched fine-grained structure. For the fine-grained known-$K$ diagnostic, the limiting object is the embedding, not an unused clustering head.

\subsection{Corruption studies clarify why zero-mask is the main line}

The distinction from scMAE is most visible in the corruption study. Original global swap corruption can be strongly harmful on Macosko, obtaining about $0.30$ ARI under the same DEC+floor backbone, while zero-mask obtains about $0.70$. Nuisance-matched swap corruption using library-size or detected-gene bins reaches a stable Macosko basin near $0.86$, and this improvement survives both known-$K$ $k$-means and a label-free Leiden check. However, the effect does not generalize. Melanoma and Quake remain within seed noise, and the tested plant datasets are flat or worse under matched shuffling. The conclusion is therefore not that zero-mask is theoretically optimal, but that it is the most stable corruption choice for the present cross-dataset spine. Matched shuffling remains a useful diagnostic for Macosko-specific structure, not the promoted method.

\subsection{Expression-derived auxiliary structure is not the stable mechanism}

Several ablations constrain the paper's claim. NeighborMix with DEC and the variance floor increases latent dimension usage but reduces Macosko ARI relative to VarFloor-scMAE. Gene-program targets can pull the embedding toward non-cell-type axes; in the program-bottleneck ablation, a real NMF program target was worse than a row-permuted target under the same auxiliary-loss form. Decoder-bypass variants show that the full mask-to-decoder path is not simply a shortcut: removing it can improve Melanoma on average and occasionally reaches a high Macosko basin, but the route is seed-unstable on fine-grained data. These results support the chosen spine by exclusion. The reliable intervention acts directly on the geometry of the clustering latent rather than on additional structure derived from the same expression matrix.

\subsection{PCA remains a necessary comparator}

A separate objective-swap check shows that forcing an assignment-prediction objective can fail even when all latent coordinates satisfy the variance floor; on Macosko, PCA+$k$-means can remain much stronger than a poorly aligned cluster-first objective under the same seeds. The formal benchmark reinforces the same point. PCA+$k$-means wins 5 of 23 paired comparisons and is the strongest available row on Macosko and Quake\_Smart-seq2\_Lung. Variance preservation is therefore necessary for the promoted DEC-scMAE path, but it is not sufficient for arbitrary clustering objectives. A submission version should keep PCA+$k$-means as a first-line baseline rather than a supplementary control.

\section{Discussion}

VarFloor-scMAE differs from scMAE in the question it answers. scMAE argues that masked reconstruction with a mask predictor can learn gene-dependent embeddings for downstream clustering. VarFloor-scMAE argues that once a clustering objective is optimized on such embeddings, latent coordinate activity becomes a first-class object of control. The formal benchmark now adds a second role: VarFloor-scMAE is a reproducible deep known-$K$ baseline that improves over PCA+$k$-means in most paired datasets, while still leaving published scMAE-family methods competitive in their overlap. The method is intentionally small: zero-mask corruption removes donor confounds, DEC supplies assignment pressure, and the standard-deviation floor prevents that pressure from deactivating coordinates.

The formula choices follow from the diagnostics. The zero-mask corruption is used because global donor shuffling can be harmful and matched donor shuffling is dataset-specific. The mask-prediction decoder is retained because removing the mask path destabilizes fine-grained training. DEC is used because post-hoc clustering alone does not test how assignment pressure reshapes the encoder. The floor is per-coordinate because the observed failure is marginal variance collapse. A covariance penalty, a graph module, or a gene-program auxiliary target would address a different hypothesis and would reintroduce the expression-derived pseudo-structure that the ablations did not support.

The method also clarifies the role of negative results. They are not evidence that scMAE cannot be improved. They specify which kinds of improvement are not yet stable: extra neighbors, external gene programs, decoder-bypass routes, and matched-swap corruption can change the embedding, but their gains are either absent, dataset-specific, or seed-unstable. The stable claim is the interaction between DEC and latent dimension usage.

\section{Limitations}

The current manuscript is still not a complete biological benchmark paper. The 23-dataset completion strengthens the computational evidence, but the protocol remains known-$K$ and does not solve unknown-$K$ model selection. The strongest mechanism table is Macosko-centered and uses seeds 42--44, while the formal seed set reports greater Macosko variability and a stronger PCA+$k$-means result on that dataset. The current evidence does not support claims about marker-gene recovery, biological program discovery, cell-type annotation, or universal transfer to plant scRNA-seq. Conditional matched-swap corruption reaches a higher Macosko basin but is not promoted because it fails the cross-dataset and plant-transfer checks. A submission-ready study requires post-hoc latent standardization controls, unknown-$K$ evaluation, biological interpretation for VarFloor-specific clusters, and a figure package that separates full-coverage paired comparisons from partial-coverage historical method rows.

\section{Conclusion}

scMAE makes masked reconstruction a representation-learning task for scRNA-seq clustering. VarFloor-scMAE studies what happens when a clustering objective is added to that task. In the mechanism diagnostic, DEC can reduce usable latent coordinate variance and damage clustering. In the formal 23-dataset benchmark, a variance-floored DEC-scMAE runner improves over PCA+$k$-means on most paired datasets, but does not uniformly outperform published scMAE-family baselines. The contribution is therefore a bounded one: a reproducible correction for a measurable representation failure, and a stronger known-$K$ deep baseline for the next frozen benchmark.
